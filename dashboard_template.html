Where electric bodas and electric cars can take hold, and where their swap stations and chargers should go

A public-data atlas of EV adoption suitability across Greater Kampala: 60 proposed battery-swap sites for e-bodas, 30 proposed fast-charging sites for e-cars, the economics for riders, drivers and car owners, and the terrain, flood and grid checks on every site. Scores rank places against each other; they are not adoption probabilities.

Built without operator data. Only 3 swap/charging stations are mapped in OpenStreetMap, and Uganda imported only 17 fully electric cars from 2022 to April 2024.

What this atlas can and cannot say

Suitability, not adoption

Indices are weighted percentile ranks of public features with judgement-based weights. The robustness layer shows how often a cell stays in the top 10% when those weights are redrawn 1,000 times. Turning scores into adoption probabilities needs operator swap volumes or registrations.

The station gap is unknown

OpenStreetMap holds almost none of the 350+ swap stations in Uganda, so the proposed network is greenfield: where to build if starting today. Once a station inventory exists, the same model treats existing stations as already built.

Proxies have blind spots

OSM activity reflects mapping effort. The wealth and home-charging proxies both come from satellite building footprints, so they move together by construction, and they misread 1 km cells that mix estates with hospitals or airstrips. WorldPop figures are modelled; 2030 is a projection.

E-cars are forward-looking

Uganda imported only 17 fully electric cars from 2022 to April 2024, so e-car demand is a scenario, not a measurement. Purchase premium, public charging price and maintenance are assumptions to replace with dealer quotes and driver surveys.

Grid data are partial

OpenStreetMap maps a small part of the UEDCL distribution network, so sites are graded by the connection their load needs, not by distance to a mapped line. Utility feeder and transformer data would turn the grid classes into a real capacity check.

Operations are scenarios

Swap volumes assume a share of Greater Kampala's 400,000 motorcycles go electric and all riders use their nearest site. Cabinet throughput, pack size and charger utilisation are assumptions. Real networks need denser sites than a coverage plan.
