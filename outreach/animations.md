# Visuals for Substack, X and LinkedIn

Everything here is generated by `make_social.py` from the model outputs, so the numbers always match the post. Every frame and image carries its data sources and "Credit: Gava Charles".

| Use | Format | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Substack post header and social preview | `figures/cover_substack.png` (1456 × 1048, 14:10) | Upload as the post's cover image |
| Substack and LinkedIn, inline | `figures/social/q*.gif` (720 × 720, 1–3 MB, loops) | Drag into the editor where the post marks them |
| X | `figures/social/q*.mp4` (1080 × 1080, H.264) | Upload the MP4, not the GIF: X keeps MP4s sharp and autoplays them |

Each animation opens on a question, shows the answer building up, and ends on a card with the punchline and a follow-up question for replies.

---

## Q1 · Where would you put Kampala's first 60 swap stations?

`q1_where_60_stations` · Sites appear one at a time on the map, coloured by whether they are petrol stations, while the share of e-boda demand reached climbs to 75%.
**Punchline:** 53 of 60 are petrol stations. *Should fuel retailers host the swap, or lose the rider?*

**X:**
> If you had to place Kampala's first 60 battery-swap stations, where would they go?
>
> I let a computer pick, one site at a time. 53 of the 60 ended up on petrol station forecourts. 🧵

**Substack:** in "The infrastructure each one needs".

## Q2 · Does an electric boda still pay 20 km from the city centre?

`q2_does_it_pay_far_out` · Bars for four distance rings grow as the network grows from 1 to 60 stations. The outer ring starts red (losing money) and turns green.
**Punchline:** Beyond 20 km, a rider loses about UGX 3,550 a day with 10 stations and gains about UGX 2,200 with 60. *Who pays for stations where commercial networks arrive last?*

**X:**
> An electric boda saves a rider about UGX 5,900 a day. But only if there's a swap station on their route.
>
> More than 20 km from Kampala's centre, with too few stations, riders would lose money.
>
> Who should pay for the stations out there?

**Substack:** in "Distance decides who benefits".

## Q3 · Going electric in Uganda: who actually comes out ahead?

`q3_who_pays_off` · Three 100-square grids fill with green (comes out ahead) or red (loses money) for a boda rider, a taxi car and a private car, from 5,000 simulations each.
**Punchline:** Bodas 100%, taxis 96%, private cars 52%. *Should incentives start with taxi fleets?*

**X:**
> Bodas 100%. Taxis 96%. Private cars 52%.
>
> That's how often going electric pays off in Uganda across 5,000 simulations each.
>
> Why is the family car a coin flip? 👇

**Substack:** in "Why cars are harder". **Lead with this one on X:** it carries the whole argument in one image.

## Q4 · Who in Kampala could charge an electric car at home?

`q4_who_can_charge_at_home` · Bars rise for the five wealth groups, from 13% in the least-wealthy fifth of neighbourhoods to 92% in the wealthiest.
**Punchline:** 13% vs 92%. *Who are public chargers for, then?*

**X:**
> Charging at home is the cheapest way to run an electric car. It's worth about UGX 1.6M a year.
>
> In Kampala's least-wealthy neighbourhoods, about 13% of households have room to do it. In the wealthiest, 92%.
>
> Who are public chargers for?

**Substack:** in "Why cars are harder".

## Q5 · If Kampala's bodas go electric, can the grid keep up?

`q5_can_the_grid_keep_up` · The share of motorcycles that are electric steps up from 10% to 25% to 50%. The peak-load counter rises to 34.9 MW, and stations turn red as they need medium-voltage connections.
**Punchline:** At 25%, 17.5 MW and 21 sites need medium voltage. *Who pays for the connections?*

**X:**
> If a quarter of Kampala's motorcycles went electric, 60 swap stations would need about 17.5 MW at peak.
>
> 21 of them would need a medium-voltage grid connection.
>
> Is the grid ready? Who pays to connect them?

**Substack:** in "The grid is the real bottleneck".

## Q6 · When bodas go electric, what happens to the petrol station?

`q6_petrol_station` · As adoption rises to 50%, one bar shows petrol no longer sold (up to 325,000 litres a day, about UGX 2.2 bn) and another shows rider visits to forecourt swap sites.
**Punchline:** Host the swap, or lose the rider. *Will Uganda's fuel retailers move first?*

**X:**
> Every electric boda is a customer who stops buying petrol. At 50% adoption in Kampala, that's about 325,000 litres a day.
>
> But 53 of the 60 best swap sites are petrol stations.
>
> Fuel retailers: host the swap, or lose the rider?

**Substack:** in "So what happens to the petrol station?".
