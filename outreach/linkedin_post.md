Kampala's electric boda boom has a geography problem.

I mapped where electric bodas and electric cars are most likely to take hold across Greater Kampala, proposed where battery-swap stations and fast chargers should go, and asked who those networks would serve. Everything is built from open data: OpenStreetMap, WorldPop, Microsoft building footprints, Sentinel-2 imagery and a 30 m elevation model.

What I found:

🔋 A rider with a swap station on their route saves about UGX 5,900 a day by going electric. That held in all 5,000 simulations.

📍 But measured from where people live, the saving depends on how dense the network is: about UGX 2,000 a day with 10 stations, 3,850 with 30 and 4,500 with 60.

🏙️ More than 20 km from the centre, riders would lose money with only 10 stations. That's where 55% of new residents are settling.

⛽ 53 of the 60 best swap sites are petrol stations. Fuel forecourts alone can carry the network.

⚡ The grid is the bottleneck: at 25% adoption, 21 of 60 stations would need medium-voltage connections.

🚕 For cars, start with taxis: an electric ride-hail car repays a UGX 12M premium in about 8 months. A private car is a coin flip.
