Why are Uganda's bodas going electric while its cars aren't?

Uganda's swap network more than doubled, from about 150 stations in mid-2024 to over 350 by late 2025 (World Bank). Over 2022 to April 2024, the country imported just 17 fully electric cars.

Both are "EVs", but they live by different economics. I mapped both across Greater Kampala using only open data: OpenStreetMap, WorldPop, Microsoft building footprints, Sentinel-2 imagery and a 30 m elevation model.

What I found:

🔋 A boda rider with a swap station on their route saves about UGX 5,900 a day. That held in all 5,000 simulations. They never buy the battery, and a swap takes minutes.

🚗 A private electric car is a coin flip: it pays off over five years in only 52% of simulations. The buyer pays for the battery up front, plus a 25% import duty.

🚕 A taxi or ride-hail car is different: it repays a UGX 12M premium in about 8 months. Uganda's first electric-car market is the working car.

🏠 Home charging is the cheapest way to run an e-car, and about 13% of households in the least-wealthy neighbourhoods could do it, against 92% in the wealthiest.

📍 For bodas, distance decides: more than 20 km from the centre, riders lose money with only 10 stations. And more than 10 km out is where 55% of new residents are settling.

⛽ 53 of the 60 best swap sites are petrol stations.

⚡ The grid is the bottleneck: at 25% boda adoption, 21 of 60 stations would need medium-voltage connections.
